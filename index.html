Research & Portfolio Work

With an academic background in Management Engineering (BSc and MSc), Economics (MA), and a Ph.D. in Finance, Nico specializes in asset pricing and macro-financial dynamics. He designs and manages multi-asset portfolios and develops quantitative models using fundamental, price, technical, and volatility data to analyze and invest across various assets. His tactical overlay leverages cross-asset trade idea generation, directional options strategies, and volatility positioning, supported by deep knowledge of options pricing, delta hedging, and market microstructure. With experience in macro market regimes and statistical return modeling, Nico integrates macroeconomic correlations and risk analytics to seek alpha and hedge against downside risk.

Recent applied research

Forecasting Stock Returns with Bayesian Econometrics and Machine Learning

This project builds a Bayesian framework for forecasting stock returns using time-varying parameters, stochastic volatility, and selective shrinkage priors. It adapts to unstable predictive relations and integrates light machine-learning layers for residual nonlinearities. The model delivers robust, uncertainty-aware forecasts and improved portfolio decisions under changing market conditions.

Dynamic Regime Transition Probabilities and Macro-Calibrated Portfolio Allocation

This study introduces a probabilistic framework for forecasting monthly macro regimes and building adaptive portfolios. A time-varying transition model, calibrated with macro and sentiment factors, generates regime probabilities that guide allocation. Using ridge-VAR forecasts and monotone probability calibration, the approach achieves reliable predictions and robust cross-asset performance, linking regime analysis to practical portfolio design.

Papers

What’s News with You: Price Forecasting and Sentiment Scores (joint work with Leone, F., Marazzina, D.)

We adopt deep learning models to forecast stock prices and to compute news sentiment scores. The aim is to determine the predictive power of the sentiment for the Dow Jones Industrial Average index price. More precisely, we employ N-HiTS, a neural network architecture designed for time series forecasting, and RoBERTa, a powerful tool in sentiment analysis, to predict next-day closing prices exploiting sentiment scores and historical closing prices. We find that the N-HiTS model, when trained with comprehensive sentiment data from all news, significantly outperforms all the considered models across all key metrics. A model trained exclusively with ESG news sentiment improves upon the baseline model in the directional accuracy, delivering the best-predicting power of the price downward movements.

Profit or Planet? Both! ESG Drivers of Efficient Portfolios and the Costs of Disclosure

This study applies an asset pricing perspective to identify key ESG indicators to construct sustainable high-return (best) portfolios. I integrate ESG variables using the Fama-French Five-Factor model (FF5) and machine learning. FF5 results show the inherent heterogeneity of ESG materiality across time and regions. The customized random forest highlights material financial and ESG factors for high-return portfolios. Key drivers of excess returns include governance (board independence, executive compensation), social factors (employee satisfaction, diversity policies), and environmental variables (GHG emissions, renewable energy use). Finally, I assess whether ESG factors predict financial fundamentals and examine the associated costs of ESG disclosure. I find that governance practices predict higher returns on assets and cash flow generation. On the other hand, ESG disclosure seems to affect capital expenditures and investment negatively. The results support the idea that non-financial disclosure is costly in the short term but yields long-term benefits.

No Gambling Here! A New Comprehensive Risk Index

I create an index that measures the risk of investment funds using a traditional econometrics tool (PCA) and a machine learning algorithm (autoencoder). The composite index measure follows the classical signaling method of credit ratings. I include financial risk variables, ESG ratings, macroeconomic variables, and fund characteristics to obtain a composite measure to categorize fund risk. The customized autoencoder performs better than the PCA allowing us to obtain an index that contains multiple sources of information and respects the directionality of the variables concerning risk. Therefore, I contribute to the risk management and decisional process for the investment funds evaluation by introducing a more complete and comprehensive measure of risk.

Climate Shocks and Corporate Resilience: Extreme Weather Events and EU Solidarity Funds for Adaptation and Mitigation

I study the effects of Extreme Weather Events on the corporate value of European Small and Medium-sized Enterprises. Additionally, I evaluate whether firms insure against natural disasters. Indeed, I study the effects of the European Solidarity Funds trying to uncover the effectiveness of these funds for climate change adaptation and risk prevention. The existing literature extensively studies the interactions between climate events and financial markets. The empirical literature mainly focuses on the pricing of climate risks for different asset classes. Furthermore, it explores the propagation and amplification of shocks along the supply chain. Conversely, this study contemplates the effect of a broader set of events on the performance of European SMEs and studies the effectiveness of the European Solidarity Funds. Our first estimate proposes a difference-in-differences (DiD) strategy in which the event is the exogenous shock for the treated group. I should consider that there may be a spatial correlation in the treatment.

Should I Stay or Should I Go? Transfers, Trade Gains, and Capital Accumulation in a Fiscal Union

We study the attainability of a fiscal union model for countries in the European Monetary Union (EMU). Recent research typically limits risk-sharing in a monetary union by introducing limited commitment frictions. Conversely, this paper contemplates productivity shocks for countries in the currency union to evaluate the viability of a fiscal union. The research proposes two models. Firstly, we propose a multi-country static model where countries share risk internationally through state-contingent transfers. In this setting, we compare welfare effects from transfers and trade gains for each country of the EMU. Therefore, we aim to establish whether the trade gains in the currency union compensate for the loss of transfers in the fiscal union, especially for high-productivity and high-income countries. The second is a dynamic model where taxes and subsidies of the EU central government redistribute wealth across countries. The value-added of this dynamic model is the evaluation of the sustainability of the transfers in the fiscal union. Additionally, the government enforces capital accumulation to increase the future productivity of a low productive country and therefore overcomes moral hazard issues. Therefore, we expect to find that, in addition to the trade gains and the multiple social benefits, remaining in the union might represent an insurance mechanism against future shocks for all the members.

Climate Risk and Brand Capital (joint work with Giudici, G., Konstantios, D.)

This study investigates the relationship between firm-level climate risk and brand capital. Analyzing an international panel of 5,862 firms from 2001-2022, we find that firms exposed to climate risk tend to have reduced brand capital. Our analysis indicates that the value of a marginal dollar of cash on brand value is significantly lower in firms with high climate risk exposure compared to those with low exposure. Additionally, the adverse impact of climate risk on brand capital is more pronounced for firms with higher levels of brand capital. We employ a quasi-natural experiment centered on the Paris Agreement to establish causality. Our findings remain robust across various estimation techniques that account for endogeneity, self-selection, and model sensitivity.
